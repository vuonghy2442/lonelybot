# The no-pile-to-pile theorem — informal writeup

This is the dedicated writeup for the engine's biggest unproven-but-validated
claim: that dropping general tableau↔tableau moves (keeping only surface
`Reveal`s) preserves solvability. The key idea, which makes everything click:
**given the suit symmetry, the only per-card facts that matter are whether a
card is stacked [on the foundation], or whether it is the top of a hidden
card.** Everything else about a card's tableau position is arrangement — and
a move that only changes arrangement cannot change solvability.

Line references are pinned to the repo state after commit `2614234`.
Legend: [x] established here / [~] argued, needs rigor / [ ] open.

## 0. What is claimed

Let the *concrete game* be standard Klondike: explicit piles, runs movable
between piles as units, deals from stock to waste. Let the *abstract game*
be the engine: the state `(stack, hidden counts, deck order+offset, visible
set)` with the five moves, generated by `gen_moves::<false>` — i.e. **with
all dominances off** (`dom_sm = 0`) and no pruner. Keeping the dominances
out of the statement matters: they narrow further, with their own
soundness story ([pruning_dominance_interaction.md](pruning_dominance_interaction.md)).

- **Completeness [~].** Every concrete winning play has an abstract winning
  counterpart (§4).
- **Concretization [~].** Every abstract winning play can be realized by a
  concrete winning play (§5).
- **Corollary (α-invariance [~]).** Solvability is a function of the
  abstract state alone — the soundness of the 61-bit transposition-table
  conflation, i.e. the state-level content of the H3 hazard in the
  interaction doc.

Together: a deal is solvable iff the raw engine solves it. The dominances
and pruners then restrict inside that equivalence.

## 1. The two games and the abstraction map

The abstraction map α sends a concrete position to its visible set, its
foundation heights, its stock order and waste offset, and the per-pile
count of unrevealed structure cards.

Two clarifications that prevent standard confusions:

- **Hidden identities are pinned, not conflated.** In the engine, a pile's
  hidden structure is always a prefix of the original deal, so within one
  game the per-pile counts determine exactly which cards are hidden where
  and in what order. The encode's amnesia about identities is therefore
  harmless *within* a game — the transposition table only ever compares
  states of the same game. No theorem is needed for this part.
- **The deal/waste partition is modeled by the offset.** A concrete
  sequence of deals is not a move in the abstract game; instead
  `deck_mask = compute_mask(false)` (src/deck.rs:212) enumerates every card
  that could be drawn *given some amount of dealing*, and drawing it jumps
  the offset directly (§4, case 1d).

## 2. The state-sufficiency view (guiding slogan — not a lemma)

The view that makes everything click: given the suit symmetry, the
solvability-relevant facts about each card reduce to:

1. whether it is **stacked** on the foundation;
2. whether it is **locked** — the surface card sitting on top of hidden
   cards (only surfaces have a position-dependent power: they are the only
   cards whose departure reveals something);
3. for hidden cards, the per-pile counts (which, given the deal, pin
   identities — §1);
4. the deck order and offset.

Everything else — which visible pile a card is on, what covers it, what it
covers — is *arrangement*.

A health warning about circularity, which formalization would force anyway:
the formal content of "arrangement beyond {stacked, locked} cannot change
solvability" **is** the theorem of §0 — an α-fiber contains *arbitrary*
arrangements of the visible cards, which twin swaps alone do not span. So
this section is motivation, not a step in the proof. The proof structure is:
§3 makes "recoverable existentially" a lemma about guards; §4 (completeness)
and §5 (concretization) make "fungible" a theorem; α-invariance then falls
out as a corollary. Within that proof, twin-swap is needed only in its
narrow scope — substituting one twin for the other when the parity
machinery has promised an uncovered card of a type — never to span
arbitrary permutations.

The slogan, for which §4–§5 supply the proof:

> **A tableau move that relocates only placed cards changes none of the
> facts 1–4 — it is invisible to α — so it cannot change what the rest of a
> winning play can do.**

That is the entire content of "no pile-to-pile": pile-to-pile moves of
*placed* cards are the moves α cannot see, and the one pile-to-pile move α
*can* see — relocating a run whose bottom card is a locked surface — is
exactly the engine's `Reveal`.

## 3. The realizability invariant [~]

Call an abstract state **realizable** if some concrete position maps to it
under α. Realizability is the invariant that makes the parity formulas
truthful:

**Parity lemma.** In any concrete arrangement, every placed card of type
`t−4` sits on some card of type `t` (that is what placing means). Hence for
each type `t`:

```
uncovered_t = present_t − placed_{t−4}        (≥ 0 in any realizable state)
```

and the `bm` formula (`get_bottom_mask`, src/state.rs:115) computes exactly
`uncovered_t > 0` on realizable states: `xor_vis ^ (xor_free << 4)` (per
type: `xor_vis[t] ^ xor_free[t−4]`) tracks the parity of the difference and
`| !(or_free << 4)` handles the zero-placed case; `& or_vis` excludes
absent types. Worked check: present 2, placed 1 → one uncovered → `bm = 1`;
present 1, placed 1 → covered → `bm = 0`.

**Realizability is preserved by every abstract move.** Induction over the
raw generator's guards — each guard is precisely the witness requirement:

| guard | witness it guarantees |
|---|---|
| `pile_stack = bm & vis & sm` | some arrangement has the card uncovered, and the foundation accepts it |
| `deck_pile = deck_mask & free_slot` | the card is drawable with some dealing, and some arrangement has an uncovered run-parent to land on |
| `stack_pile = swap_pair(sm>>4) & free_slot` | the foundation top has an uncovered landing spot in some arrangement |
| `reveal = vis & locked & free_slot & …` | the departing surface has a destination in some arrangement |
| `deck_stack = deck_mask & sm` | drawable and stackable — no arrangement needed |

The two ¬-arrangement facts a `Reveal` needs — that the moved card is the
surface of its pile and that the pile's structure then shrinks — are
recorded by the hidden counts, which the move updates. [The one gap in the
table is the first-layer-king exclusion inside `reveal`; see §6.]

**Invariant maintenance (the proof the table glosses).** The invariant
"every placed card of type `t−4` sits on a type-`t` card (a placed King
sits on an empty pile)" must be *created and preserved* by each move:

- placements — `DeckPile`, `StackPile`, and the departing card of a
  `Reveal` — all require `free_slot`, i.e. an uncovered parent, so the
  incoming card lands on a legal parent;
- `PileStack` removes a top, uncovering exactly the card beneath — the
  covered and present counts of the type drop together;
- `Reveal(X)` converts X from structure to placed and lands it on its
  `free_slot` witness;
- `DeckStack` places nothing on the tableau.

Boundary conditions, so nobody trips later: (a) Aces are never covered —
no valid `t−4` exists below the Ace types; (b) a placed King covers
nothing — it sits on an empty pile, and there is no type above King whose
`placed_{t−4}` count it could enter (the `<<4` shift lands in bits that
are never set). The deck side of the guards has an executable
differential test already: `test_draw_unrolling` (src/state.rs:624) walks
random games asserting that the drawable-position enumeration matches the
deck's per-position `Drawable` annotations at every state.

## 4. Completeness: concrete winning play ⇒ abstract winning play [~]

Transform the concrete play move by move.

**Case 1 — moves α tracks directly.**

- (a) `PileStack` (including reveal-by-stacking a locked card): maps to the
  abstract `PileStack`. The concrete position is itself the witness for
  `bm & vis & sm` (the card is a concrete top, hence its type has an
  uncovered card; §3's parity lemma).
- (b) `StackPile` (worry back): maps to abstract `StackPile`; the concrete
  landing spot is the `free_slot` witness. Note the raw generator has no
  `!dom_sm` — the worry-back ban is a dominance, out of scope here.
- (c) draws to pile/foundation: map to `DeckPile`/`DeckStack`.
- (d) **deals**: delete. Deals only advance the offset, and the offset is
  (re)established by the next draw's position jump. Intervening non-deck
  moves do not consult the offset, and the win condition does not either.
  (If the play never draws again, the offset drift is irrelevant.)

**Case 2 — a run relocation whose bottom card X is *placed*.** Delete it.
No component of the abstract state changes — no foundation push/pop, no
hidden pop, no deck change, no visible-set change — so the abstract state
trajectory is literally unaltered. Concretely this move rearranged;
abstractly nothing happened.

**Compression lemma (why deletion is safe, per move).** "Abstract legality
is a state function" alone would not answer a concrete-precondition
question — after the deletions there may be no coherent concrete
trajectory at all. What closes the gap: guards are checked against
*abstract* states, and each abstract state along the transformed play is
still realized by the **original** concrete play's position at that step
(case-2 deletions never changed α-images); the original concrete legality
of the move at that position is exactly the witness property the guard
demands (§3's table). So each surviving move is abstract-generated —
checked one move at a time, with no surviving concrete trajectory needed.

**Case 3 — a run relocation whose bottom card X is a locked surface.** Map
it to `Reveal(X)`: identical abstract effect — the structure under X pops,
the revealed card joins the visible set, X leaves the locked set and stays
visible, the run above X stays visible and placed. The destination the
concrete move used is the `free_slot` witness.

**Case 4 — the bottom X is a first-layer king.** The raw generator refuses
`Reveal(X)` here; handled by the relabeling lemma (§6).

The transformed play reaches the all-stacked state — the win condition is
a state function — so it wins. ∎ (modulo the fiddly parts flagged in §5/§6)

## 5. Concretization: abstract winning play ⇒ concrete winning play [ ]

The harder direction, and the one that decides whether this document is a
proof plan or a proof. The reshaping moves available inside an α-fiber are
**not** free rearrangements: concretely you can only relocate a *complete
valid run* onto a *legal uncovered parent* (or a king to an empty pile). A
needed card may be buried under a placed blocker whose own legal
destinations are themselves covered — a *blocker chain*.

The **primary case** the reshape lemma must handle — and the only point
where the set-based generator can emit a move with no matching concrete
move in the current arrangement — is the twin expansion: `bm` is
type-level, and its `* 0b11` re-expansion puts *both* twin bits into
`pile_stack`/`free_slot`, so the generator may pick the twin that is
concretely covered while its sibling is the uncovered one. Concretizing
that move requires either reshaping to expose the right twin, or a
twin-swap substitution in the play. This is where twin-swap actually earns
its keep — in this narrow scope, not spanning arbitrary permutations.

Two things would upgrade this section from "rich enough" to a proof:

1. **A normal-form strategy.** Fix a canonical arrangement per abstract
   state — the one `compute_visible_piles` (src/state.rs) constructs —
   and prove that every fiber element reshapes to it, or at least reshapes
   enough to expose each guard-witness. A single total order on fiber
   elements collapses "rich enough" into an existence proof with a
   terminus.
2. **A falsifier instrument.** `convert_move` (src/convert.rs:9) currently
   concretizes each abstract move with at most one extra relocation, and
   its callers `unwrap()` (src/convert.rs:138,153) — a depth-1 chain that
   ever failed would have panicked there. Turn that into a *measurement*:
   replace the unwraps with a counted error and log every abstract move
   whose concretization needs more than K relocations. Zero logs over the
   solved corpus = the reshape lemma with measured depth ≤ K; a hit = the
   exact counterexample shape to hand to Lean as a `sorry`-free target.

Empirical support meanwhile: the 1M-seed Solvitaire and Klondike-Solver
cross-checks (method.md §10) exercise this direction end-to-end on every
solved deal.

**Corollary (α-invariance) [~].** Completeness + concretization give:
solvability is a function of the abstract state alone. That is the
soundness of the 61-bit transposition-table conflation — the state-level
content of the H3 hazard in the interaction doc — and, combined with §1's
pinned hidden identities, it discharges the macro doc's open item O2
outright: the hidden-identity conflation O2 worried about does not exist
within a game.

## 6. The first-layer-king exclusion (relabeling lemma) [~]

`reveal` excludes first-layer kings (`!(first_layer & KING_MASK)`,
src/state.rs:293): the only such move is relocating a lone king that
occupies an otherwise-empty structure to another empty pile — a move that
changes the abstract state (the pile count goes 1 → 0) but, we claim,
nothing of consequence:

**Lemma.** Let s be the pre-state (pile P = [K] only, another pile P′
empty) and s′ the post-state (P empty, K placed on P′). Within the engine's
move set, s and s′ are behaviorally isomorphic up to swapping the roles of
P and P′:

- K is a landing spot in both (a top with nothing beneath in both);
- the empty pile exists in both;
- K's mobility is identical: `PileStack(K)` when stackable; `Reveal(K)` is
  unavailable in both — excluded in s by the mask, impossible in s′ because
  K is placed — and any concrete [K + run] relocation maps to the same
  abstract moves in both;
- all other piles are untouched.

So the engine keeping only s loses nothing: any winning play through s′
translates to one through s. [The isomorphism should be re-checked against
the pruner contexts (e.g. the forced-king rule after `(Reveal, RevealEmpty)`
fires equally in both — the empty pile exists in both states).]

## 7. What this theorem does *not* cover

- The **dominances** (method.md §5) and the **pruners** (§6): they restrict
  inside the raw equivalence established here; their story is the
  [interaction doc](pruning_dominance_interaction.md). Note in particular
  that §6's relabeling lemma is an isomorphism of *raw* games: under
  `FullPruner` the two states differ in path history (one carries the
  `(Reveal, RevealEmpty)` kings-only context), so the pruner layer needs a
  *context-sensitive* version of that equivalence, not the state-level one
  — resolving it at the wrong layer would silently change what the pruner
  may assume.
- The **twin-swap theorem** itself (macro doc §3): used here in its narrow
  scope — twin substitution in the reshape lemma — not as a claim that
  twin swaps span arrangement space.
- The **random/no-undo** game: this writeup is about the thoughtful setting.

## 8. Relation to Blake & Gent

Their Theorem 4 game class *includes* pile-to-pile moves ("moves of
complete or incomplete built piles as a single move… the only place a card
can move from the tableau is to another tableau pile or to a foundation").
This theorem removes exactly those moves from the picture, which is why it
is the bridge that makes their Appendix B proofs portable to this engine
(the blocker named in the interaction doc §8.2): once the concrete and
abstract games are equivalent with the raw move set, porting their Theorem 1
(safe moves) and Theorem 4/5 (immediate building, compatibility) is a matter
of the set-abstraction port, not of the move set.

## 9. Lean obligations

1. Realizability invariant + the parity lemma (§3), including the
   maintenance cases and boundary conditions — mechanical arithmetic.
2. The compression lemma (§4, case 2) — near-trivial once positions are
   quotiented by α; the Lean statement is the per-move witness argument.
3. The relabeling isomorphism (§6) — small; raw-level only (the
   context-sensitive extension belongs to the pruner layer, §7).
4. The reshape lemma for concretization (§5) — the one worthwhile fight:
   the twin-expansion case first, then the normal-form conjecture, with
   the relocation-depth instrument as the falsifier.
